Sources: The UI/UX Playbook (uxpeak; IDs A-… to H-…, page numbers are PDF pages) and, for W-… IDs, paraphrased tips from public UX pattern pages (cited as web:<slug>, no page). Generated by scripts/build-knowledge.mjs — do not edit by hand.

## Web patterns: Motion

### W1-01 Run entrances at 200–300ms with ease-out  (src: animation-timing)
- **Rule**: Time entrance animations between 200 and 300ms and use a cubic ease-out so they decelerate into place. Longer than about 300ms starts to feel sluggish.
- **Why**: This range reads as responsive yet deliberate.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use ease-out for entering elements. Don't use linear easing or stretch entrances past ~300ms.
- **Audit check**: grep `transition`/`animation` durations on enter states (`duration-500`, `duration-700`, `0.5s`+); grep `linear` or `ease-in` on entrances.
- **Restyle fix**: Set entrance transitions to 200–300ms with `cubic-bezier` ease-out (e.g. `ease-out`, `duration-200`/`duration-300`).
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W1-02 Make exits faster than entrances  (src: animation-timing)
- **Rule**: Dismissals should run quicker than the matching entrance, e.g. ~250ms in and ~150ms out (roughly 40% faster). Symmetric timing makes the UI feel like it drags.
- **Why**: Quick exits make dismissals feel snappy.
- **Do / Don't**: Do shorten exit durations relative to entrance. Don't use identical in/out timing.
- **Audit check**: Compare enter vs leave durations in modals, toasts, menus (`data-[state=closed]:duration-*`, `exit` transition props, `leave` classes); flag equal values.
- **Restyle fix**: Set exit duration to ~60% of the entrance (e.g. 250ms in / 150ms out).
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W1-03 Respond to taps within 100ms  (src: animation-timing)
- **Rule**: Taps and presses need a visible response in less than 100ms; a slower reaction feels laggy even when the underlying action completes instantly.
- **Why**: Late acknowledgement makes the interface seem unresponsive.
- **Do / Don't**: Do make `:active`/press transitions brief. Don't let sluggish transitions hold back pressed states.
- **Audit check**: Check `:active`, `active:` and button `transition-duration` values; flag > 100ms on press feedback.
- **Restyle fix**: Set press/active state transitions to ≤100ms (e.g. `duration-75`).
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: motion, interaction-cost, buttons

### W1-04 Stagger list items about 50ms apart  (src: animation-timing)
- **Rule**: Offset each list item's entrance by about 50ms. At 30ms the items blur into one blob; at 100ms the list crawls in.
- **Why**: A good stagger lets the eye follow items without slowing the page.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use ~50ms increments. Don't use 30ms or 100ms steps.
- **Audit check**: grep `animation-delay`, `transition-delay`, `stagger`, `delay-[…]`, or `index * ` multipliers in motion code; check step is ≈50ms.
- **Restyle fix**: Set per-item delay to `index * 50ms`.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W1-05 Reserve long bouncy motion for attention moments  (src: animation-timing)
- **Rule**: Attention-grabbing elements (notifications are one example) may run 500–800ms with a bounce or overshoot. Spring and bounce belong only to moments that genuinely need attention; routine entrances use ease-out.
- **Why**: Overusing bounce dilutes its signal and makes routine UI feel playful or slow.
- **Do / Don't**: Do reserve spring/bounce for moments that genuinely need attention (a notification is one case). Don't apply bounce easing to ordinary entrances or controls.
- **Audit check**: grep `bounce`, `spring`, `overshoot`, `cubic-bezier` with values >1 on routine components; do not flag deliberate restrained springs on presses/modals/confirmations (see W2-02).
- **Restyle fix**: Replace bounce on routine entrances (menus, panels, list items) with ease-out; keep springs on attention moments and on the restrained press/modal/confirmation springs from W2-02.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W1-06 Animate accordion height with grid rows, not height:auto  (src: accordion-disclosure)
- **Rule**: `height: auto` cannot transition and just snaps. Animate `grid-template-rows` from `0fr` to `1fr` on a `display: grid` wrapper, or measure `scrollHeight` and animate to a pixel value.
- **Why**: Collapsible panels otherwise jump instead of easing open.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use the 0fr→1fr grid technique or a measured height. Don't expect `transition: height` to work from/to auto.
- **Audit check**: grep `transition.*height` combined with `height: auto` / `h-auto` on collapsible panels; look for absence of `grid-template-rows` / `grid-rows-[0fr]`.
- **Restyle fix**: Wrap panel content in a grid container and toggle `grid-template-rows: 0fr` ↔ `1fr` (inner `overflow: hidden`).
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W1-07 Sync the chevron with the panel animation  (src: accordion-disclosure)
- **Rule**: Give the chevron rotation and the panel height an identical duration and easing curve. A lag of even ~10 frames between them looks broken.
- **Why**: A single coordinated motion looks fluid, not janky.
- **Do / Don't**: Do share one duration and easing between icon and panel. Don't let the chevron fall behind the panel.
- **Audit check**: Compare `transition` duration/easing on the chevron (`rotate-180`, `transform`) vs the panel; flag mismatches.
- **Restyle fix**: Apply identical duration and easing tokens to both.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, icons, craft

### W1-08 Open dropdown menus in about 150ms  (src: dropdown-design)
- **Rule**: Make a dropdown's opening animation last about 150ms: 50ms seems abrupt and cheap, while 500ms seems slow and heavy.
- **Why**: 150ms looks smooth yet doesn't make users wait.
- **Do / Don't**: Do animate opening at ~150ms. Don't use no transition at all, or anything longer than ~150ms.
- **Audit check**: Check dropdown/popover/select content enter transitions (`duration-*`, `animation-duration`) for 0 or ≥300ms.
- **Restyle fix**: Set menu enter animation to ~150ms ease-out.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

## Web patterns: Fundamentals

### W1-09 Build nested radii as outer minus padding  (src: border-radius)
- **Rule**: For a rounded element inside another, inner radius = outer radius − padding so the curves stay concentric.
- **Why**: Concentric corners look intentional; mismatched ones look "off".
- **Do / Don't**: Do compute the inner radius from the outer one. Don't nest a rounded card in another with the same radius.
- **Audit check**: Find nested rounded containers (`rounded-xl` inside `rounded-xl` with `p-*`) and compare radii vs padding.
- **Restyle fix**: Reduce the inner `border-radius` to outer − padding (min 0).
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: craft, borders, spacing

### W1-10 Standardize corner radii on a single scale (4·8·12·16·24)  (src: border-radius)
- **Rule**: Pull all corner radii from one shared scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24) rather than choosing values component by component.
- **Why**: Uniformity is what gives a UI its polished look.
- **Do / Don't**: Do reuse the same scale across components. Don't invent a new radius for each component.
- **Audit check**: grep `border-radius` / `rounded-[` values not in {4,8,12,16,24}; count distinct radii.
- **Restyle fix**: Snap off-scale radii to the nearest scale step and expose them as tokens.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: craft, borders

### W1-11 Grow radius with surface size and fit brand tone  (src: border-radius)
- **Rule**: Radius rises with surface size: ~4px tooltips, ~8px inputs, ~12px cards, ~16px modals, ~24px panels. Sharp corners feel corporate, round ones friendly; choose per brand.
- **Why**: Radius signals both size hierarchy and personality.
- **Do / Don't**: Do raise radius as surfaces get larger. Don't put an oversized playful radius on a serious brand, or a tight one on a playful brand.
- **Audit check**: Compare radius of buttons/inputs/cards/modals; flag a modal with smaller radius than an input, or a 24px+ radius on small controls.
- **Restyle fix**: Assign radii by component size per the scale above.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: craft, borders, hierarchy

### W1-12 Check every text/background pair against WCAG contrast  (src: color-accessibility)
- **Rule**: Contrast is a ratio set by the background. Aim for 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text; below 3:1 text becomes effectively invisible.
- **Why**: The same color can be crisp on one surface and vanish on another.
- **Do / Don't**: Do test each text-on-background pair. Don't assume a color is fine because it worked elsewhere.
- **Audit check**: Compute contrast for every text color against its actual background; flag <4.5:1 body, <3:1 large text.
- **Restyle fix**: Darken or lighten the text (or background) until the ratio clears the threshold.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: contrast, accessibility, color

### W1-13 Raise contrast on muted gray text first  (src: color-accessibility)
- **Rule**: Low contrast mostly lurks in muted grays: secondary headings, card labels and navigation links often measure just 1.5–2:1.
- **Why**: Looking sleek doesn't make text easier to read.
- **Do / Don't**: Do darken or lighten muted secondary text until it passes. Don't leave low-contrast gray on navigation links and labels.
- **Audit check**: Check `text-gray-400`/`text-slate-400`/`opacity-50` on light backgrounds, and nav/label colors, for ratio <4.5:1.
- **Restyle fix**: Step muted text to a darker (light mode) or lighter (dark mode) token that meets 4.5:1.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: contrast, accessibility, typography

### W1-14 Pair every color cue with a second signal  (src: color-accessibility)
- **Rule**: Never convey meaning by color alone; ~8% of users have color vision deficiency and red/green merge. Add an icon, label, trend arrow or chart pattern next to each color cue.
- **Why**: The message must survive when the color doesn't.
- **Do / Don't**: Do add an icon or text to error/success states. Don't separate errors from success by red-vs-green only.
- **Audit check**: Inspect error/success/status elements for color-only styling (`text-red-*`, `text-green-*`, colored dots) with no icon/text/aria.
- **Restyle fix**: Add an icon (and label text) to each state and arrows to up/down stats.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: accessibility, color, states, icons

### W1-15 Avoid pure black backgrounds in dark mode  (src: dark-mode)
- **Rule**: Build dark mode on a near-black base such as #121212 rather than #000000 so shadows and elevation stay visible.
- **Why**: Pure black flattens depth.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use a near-black grey base. Don't use #000 as the page background.
- **Audit check**: grep `#000`, `#000000`, `bg-black`, `rgb(0,0,0)` as dark-theme backgrounds.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace with ~#121212 and lighten raised surfaces.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: dark-mode, depth, color

### W1-16 Express dark-mode elevation with lighter surfaces  (src: dark-mode)
- **Rule**: Raise each layer of elevation with a progressively lighter grey (base, then surface, then elevated); lightness takes over the job that shadows do in light themes.
- **Why**: On dark backgrounds a shadow is nearly invisible, so brightness has to signal depth.
- **Do / Don't**: Do define a token per layer. Don't paint the page, cards and modals one identical dark colour.
- **Audit check**: Compare background colors of page, card, popover in dark theme; flag identical values.
- **Restyle fix**: Define base/surface/elevated tokens with increasing lightness and apply per layer.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: dark-mode, depth, color

### W1-17 Desaturate accents ~20% in dark mode  (src: dark-mode)
- **Rule**: Cut accent saturation about 20% on dark backgrounds, since fully saturated buttons and highlights vibrate and tire the eyes.
- **Why**: Toned-down accents look more refined on dark UI.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use a toned-down accent variant in dark theme. Don't carry the light-mode saturated accent over unchanged.
- **Audit check**: Compare accent values between light and dark themes; flag identical highly saturated accents (`dark:` variants absent).
- **Restyle fix**: Add a dark-theme accent token ~20% less saturated.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: dark-mode, color

### W1-18 Soften text white and tier it with opacity in dark mode  (src: dark-mode)
- **Rule**: Don't set text to #FFFFFF on dark UI; use a soft off-white, and build hierarchy by stepping white opacity down for high, medium and disabled emphasis.
- **Why**: Pure white glares and fatigues the eyes; opacity tiers keep hierarchy without new hues.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use opacity levels for emphasis. Don't use #FFF for all text.
- **Audit check**: grep `#fff`, `#ffffff`, `text-white` on dark backgrounds, esp. for body text.
- **Restyle fix**: Use an off-white body color and `rgba(255,255,255,α)` tiers for secondary/disabled text.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: dark-mode, typography, hierarchy, contrast

### W1-19 Build shadows from three stacked layers  (src: depth-layers)
- **Rule**: Combine three shadows: a tight ~2px one, a medium ~12px spread and a broad ~32px ambient one, imitating how real light falls away.
- **Why**: A stack looks more physical than a single flat shadow.
- **Do / Don't**: Do layer several shadows whose blur and offset grow. Don't depend on one `box-shadow`.
- **Audit check**: grep `box-shadow` / `shadow-*` on cards and popovers; flag single-layer shadows.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace with a three-layer `box-shadow` (≈2px / 12px / 32px blurs).
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: shadows, depth, craft

### W1-20 Add subtle hover lift with border emphasis  (src: depth-layers)
- **Rule**: Lift hovered elements toward the viewer with `translateZ` plus about `scale(1.03)` and a soft glow, and brighten the border/shadow intensity to reinforce it.
- **Why**: A small physical reaction makes elements feel responsive without cartoonish exaggeration.
- **Do / Don't**: Do keep lifts to a few px and ~3% scale. Don't push scale or parallax so far it steals attention.
- **Audit check**: grep `hover:scale-*`, `:hover { transform: … scale(` values >1.05 and missing border/shadow change.
- **Restyle fix**: Use ~`scale(1.03)` plus a border/shadow intensity change on hover.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: depth, shadows, interaction-cost, motion

### W1-21 Vary parallax speed per layer, subtly  (src: depth-layers)
- **Rule**: Move parallax layers at unequal rates to suggest distance: slowest in the background, moderate in the middle, quickest in front (about 1x / 2.5x / 5x).
- **Why**: Unequal speeds imply genuine depth.
- **Do / Don't**: Do give each layer its own scroll speed. Don't push offsets so far that they draw focus from the content.
- **Audit check**: Check scroll-linked transforms (`translateY` bound to scroll, `data-speed`, `parallax`) for a single shared speed or extreme values.
- **Restyle fix**: Assign slower speed to back layers and faster to front, keeping offsets modest.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: depth, motion, craft

## Web patterns: System

### W1-22 Replace hardcoded values with semantic tokens  (src: design-system-kit)
- **Rule**: Replace raw hex colors with semantic tokens such as `var(--brand)` or `var(--error)`; intent then outlives redesigns, and renaming one token updates the entire app.
- **Why**: Tokens act as the one source of truth for consistency.
- **Do / Don't**: Do define each value as a named token. Don't write hex or pixel values directly in components.
- **Audit check**: grep `#[0-9a-fA-F]{3,8}`, `rgb(`, and arbitrary `[#…]` Tailwind values in components outside the token file.
- **Restyle fix**: Move values to CSS variables/theme tokens and reference them.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: color, craft

### W1-23 Use a numbered color scale mapped to semantic names  (src: design-system-kit)
- **Rule**: Create a 100–900 numbered color scale so shades are systematic, and alias its steps to semantic names like brand, success and error.
- **Why**: Systematic shades replace luck.
- **Do / Don't**: Do define scale steps and then alias them. Don't choose individual one-off shades.
- **Audit check**: Count distinct colors in CSS vs the defined palette; flag shades outside the scale.
- **Restyle fix**: Define the scale and remap ad-hoc colors to the nearest step.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: color, craft

### W1-24 Set a fixed type scale of sizes and weights  (src: design-system-kit)
- **Rule**: Set a fixed type scale of sizes and weights. Uniform size and weight everywhere erases hierarchy.
- **Why**: A real scale lets heading and body be told apart instantly.
- **Do / Don't**: Do give each level its own size/weight pair. Don't set all text at one size and weight.
- **Audit check**: List distinct `font-size`/`font-weight` combos; flag too few distinct levels or many off-scale values.
- **Restyle fix**: Introduce a type scale and apply by role (heading, subheading, body, caption).
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: typography, hierarchy

### W1-25 Space everything in 4px steps  (src: design-system-kit)
- **Rule**: Space things on a 4px scale (space-1 = 4 through space-16 = 64). Arbitrary gaps such as 7px, 23px or 11px look careless.
- **Why**: Gaps taken from a scale come across as intentional.
- **Do / Don't**: Do take spacing from the scale. Don't eyeball gaps.
- **Audit check**: grep `padding`/`margin`/`gap` px values not divisible by 4; Tailwind arbitrary `p-[7px]` etc.
- **Restyle fix**: Snap values to the nearest 4px-scale step.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: spacing, craft

### W1-26 Describe every component by variant, size and state  (src: design-system-kit)
- **Rule**: Define each component through variants, sizes and states: buttons come as primary, secondary, ghost and destructive; sizes as small, medium and large; inputs cover default, focus, error and disabled.
- **Why**: A uniform component API keeps the interface coherent.
- **Do / Don't**: Do cover every variant and state. Don't leave states unstyled or built as one-offs.
- **Audit check**: Check buttons/inputs for missing `:focus-visible`, error and disabled styles and for ad-hoc one-off button styles.
- **Restyle fix**: Create variant/size/state classes (e.g. cva) and refactor one-offs onto them.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: buttons, forms, states, craft

### W1-27 Map easing and duration to intent  (src: design-system-kit)
- **Rule**: Use ease-out for entering, ease-in-out for moving and ease-in for leaving, with a duration scale running from 100ms for micro-interactions up to 500ms for complex transitions.
- **Why**: Intent-driven easing feels natural and consistent.
- **Do / Don't**: Do define easing and duration as tokens. Don't use arbitrary durations, or a single easing everywhere.
- **Audit check**: Collect distinct `transition-duration` and `timing-function` values; flag many one-off values or durations beyond 500ms.
- **Restyle fix**: Add motion tokens (100–500ms scale, three easings) and reference them.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W1-28 Name tokens by role, not value  (src: design-tokens)
- **Rule**: Name tokens by meaning (`color-primary`, `spacing-md`, `font-body`) rather than value (`color-blue-500`, `spacing-16`), which becomes false after a rebrand.
- **Why**: Role names survive visual changes.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use role-based names. Don't bake literal values into names.
- **Audit check**: grep token/variable names containing color words or numbers (`--blue-500` used directly in components).
- **Restyle fix**: Add semantic aliases and use them in components.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: craft, color

### W1-29 Layer tokens primitives → semantic → component  (src: design-tokens)
- **Rule**: Structure tokens in three layers (primitives, semantic, component), each referencing the layer above, so one primitive edit cascades to all dependents.
- **Why**: One edit replaces hunting down 47 values.
- **Do / Don't**: Do reference tokens from tokens. Don't hardcode raw values across components.
- **Audit check**: Inspect token file for a flat list with no aliasing; count components using raw values vs `var(--…)`.
- **Restyle fix**: Introduce semantic and component aliases referencing primitives.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: craft

### W1-30 Snap stray sizes to a fixed scale  (src: design-tokens)
- **Rule**: Define one scale and round everything onto it, so stray values such as 13px padding or a 17px gap become 12 and 16.
- **Why**: Consistency no longer relies on guessing.
- **Do / Don't**: Do round arbitrary spacing and font sizes to scale steps. Don't leave off-scale values behind.
- **Audit check**: grep `13px`, `17px`, odd `px` values in padding/gap/font-size; arbitrary Tailwind values.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace with nearest scale tokens.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: spacing, typography, craft

### W1-31 Implement dark mode as a token set swap  (src: design-tokens)
- **Rule**: Dark mode is swapping one token set for another via alias tokens, not inverting colors.
- **Why**: Same components get a deliberately different feel.
- **Do / Don't**: Do remap semantic tokens per theme. Don't use `filter: invert` or color flips.
- **Audit check**: grep `filter: invert`, many per-component `dark:` hardcoded colors vs theme variable overrides under `.dark` / `[data-theme]`.
- **Restyle fix**: Define theme token sets and have components use semantic tokens only.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: dark-mode, color, craft

## Web patterns: Inputs

### W1-32 Debounce autosave around 800ms  (src: autosave-ux)
- **Rule**: Never write on every keystroke. Reset a timer on each key and commit one write after roughly 800ms of silence.
- **Why**: One clean save avoids request floods.
- **Do / Don't**: Do debounce writes (~800ms). Don't save on each `onChange`.
- **Audit check**: grep `onChange`/`input` handlers calling `fetch`/`mutate`/`save` without `debounce`/`setTimeout`.
- **Restyle fix**: Wrap the save in a ~800ms debounce.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: forms, interaction-cost

### W1-33 Model the save indicator as explicit states  (src: autosave-ux)
- **Rule**: Run the status pill off a state machine with typing, saving, saved, offline and error states, and never show "Saved" before the write has landed.
- **Why**: Users rely on the indicator more than on the feature.
- **Do / Don't**: Do base status on real results, updated live. Don't show "Saved" for a change the server never received.
- **Audit check**: Look for a static "Saved" label or boolean `isSaved` without error/offline states.
- **Restyle fix**: Add the five states and set "Saved" only on success response.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: states, forms, copy

### W1-34 Queue edits offline and replay oldest first  (src: autosave-ux)
- **Rule**: When offline, push edits into a local queue, show a badge with the pending count, and drain the queue oldest-first on reconnect.
- **Why**: Edits aren't lost and replay order stays correct.
- **Do / Don't**: Do queue and replay in order. Don't drop edits on network loss.
- **Audit check**: grep `navigator.onLine`, `online`/`offline` listeners; check for a queue and pending badge.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a local queue plus pending-count badge.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: states, forms

### W1-35 Never silently overwrite concurrent edits  (src: autosave-ux)
- **Rule**: With two tabs on one document, last-write-wins can wipe out someone's work. Merge simultaneous changes or warn the user.
- **Why**: A silent overwrite can destroy an hour of effort.
- **Do / Don't**: Do merge or warn the user. Don't overwrite without telling anyone.
- **Audit check**: Look for version/ETag/updatedAt checks on save; absence suggests last-write-wins.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a conflict warning/merge prompt on version mismatch.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: states, forms

### W1-36 Guard unsaved work on exit  (src: autosave-ux)
- **Rule**: If unsaved changes exist, intercept tab close with the browser's `beforeunload` prompt.
- **Why**: One dialog beats retyping an afternoon of work.
- **Do / Don't**: Do confirm exit with unsaved work. Don't let the tab close silently.
- **Audit check**: grep `beforeunload` in editors/forms with dirty state.
- **Restyle fix**: Register `beforeunload` while the form is dirty.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: forms, states

### W1-37 Validate on client for speed and on server for trust  (src: behind-the-button)
- **Rule**: Use client-side validation (inline errors, green checks, 0 network calls) for instant feedback, but re-run every check stricter on the server and recompute totals from your own catalog.
- **Why**: Client requests can be forged.
- **Do / Don't**: Do validate on both sides. Don't trust client-sent prices or values.
- **Audit check**: Check API handlers for use of client-provided `price`/`total` fields and missing server validation (zod/schema).
- **Restyle fix**: Add server validation and recompute totals server-side.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: forms, craft

### W1-38 Use optimistic UI only for cheap, reversible actions  (src: behind-the-button)
- **Rule**: Likes, favorites and renames can repaint instantly and reconcile later; for money or irreversible actions hold the spinner until the server confirms. On response, repaint with server truth such as the real order ID.
- **Why**: Optimistic updates on payments misreport outcomes.
- **Do / Don't**: Do wait for server confirmation on payments. Don't use optimistic UI on irreversible actions.
- **Audit check**: grep `optimistic`, `onMutate`, `setQueryData` in checkout/payment/delete flows.
- **Restyle fix**: Show a loading state until confirmation and render server-returned data.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: states, interaction-cost

### W1-39 Use :has() instead of mirroring DOM state in JS  (src: css-has-selector)
- **Rule**: Let `:has()` style state the DOM already holds, e.g. `.plan:has(:checked)`, `.field:has(:user-invalid)`, or `form:has(:user-invalid) button { opacity: .4 }`. Every major browser has supported it since 2023.
- **Why**: It eliminates derived React state and handlers.
- **Do / Don't**: Do put the rule on the container; border, label and icon then update together. Don't copy checked/invalid state into React merely to switch a class.
- **Audit check**: Find `useState` that only toggles a class from checked/invalid/open, and absence of `:has(`/`has-[…]:` usage.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace with `:has()` / Tailwind `has-[:checked]:` rules.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: forms, states, craft

### W1-40 Prefer :user-invalid over :invalid  (src: css-has-selector)
- **Rule**: Style errors with `:user-invalid` so they show after the user leaves a field, not on first render.
- **Why**: Avoids showing errors before interaction.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use `:user-invalid`. Don't use `:invalid` that fires immediately.
- **Audit check**: grep `:invalid` / `invalid:` in field styling.
- **Restyle fix**: Switch to `:user-invalid` (or `user-invalid:`).
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: forms, states

### W1-41 Use CSS for layout and scroll-lock reactions  (src: css-has-selector)
- **Rule**: Let CSS react to DOM structure: `.app:has(aside) { grid-template-columns: 280px 1fr }` for sidebars, `.grid:has(> :nth-child(n + 5))` for quantity queries, and `body:has(dialog[open]) { overflow: hidden }` to lock scroll.
- **Why**: Removes prop threading, JS counting and cleanup effects.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use these selectors. Don't write cleanup effects or count children in JS for layout.
- **Audit check**: grep `document.body.style.overflow` in effects, `showSidebar` props, `children.length` used to choose layout.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace with equivalent `:has()` rules.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: layout, craft

### W1-42 Offer a three-state select-all checkbox  (src: bulk-actions)
- **Rule**: The header checkbox needs empty, partial (indeterminate dash) and checked states; clicking from partial should select everything, never clear.
- **Why**: Selection scope is visible at a glance.
- **Do / Don't**: Do support the indeterminate state. Don't offer a header checkbox with only two states.
- **Audit check**: grep `indeterminate` in table header checkbox code.
- **Restyle fix**: Set `indeterminate` when some rows selected; clicking from partial selects all.

## Web patterns: Motion

### W2-01 Default to ease-out for entering elements  (src: easing-curves)
- **Rule**: Entering elements should begin quickly then slow as they settle (ease-out); reserve linear easing for endless loops like spinners or marquees.
- **Why**: Slowing to rest imitates physical objects; linear timing on UI that starts and stops feels robotic.
- **Do / Don't**: Do apply ease-out to entrances. Don't apply linear timing to UI that begins and halts.
- **Audit check**: grep `transition-timing-function: linear`, `ease-linear`, `animation-timing-function: linear` on non-spinner/marquee elements; check enter animations use `ease-out`/`cubic-bezier` deceleration.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace linear timing on entering UI with ease-out (`ease-out` in Tailwind); exits use the leaving curve (ease-in) per W1-27; keep linear only for spinners and marquees.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W2-02 Add a restrained spring overshoot to presses and modals  (src: easing-curves)
- **Rule**: A slight spring overshoot on button presses, modals, and confirmations makes the UI feel alive and premium. Keep stiffness/bounce low so the element does not wobble.
- **Why**: A little overshoot reads as polish; too much reads as broken.
- **Do / Don't**: Do add subtle overshoot to presses, modals, and confirmations. Don't crank bounce until the element wobbles.
- **Audit check**: grep spring configs (`type: "spring"`, `stiffness`, `damping`, `cubic-bezier` with y>1); flag very low damping / high bounce values that produce visible oscillation.
- **Restyle fix**: Apply a single low-bounce spring (or overshoot cubic-bezier) to press/modal/confirmation transitions and raise damping if it wobbles.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W2-03 Stagger list and card entrances  (src: easing-curves)
- **Rule**: Offset list and card entrances by a few frames each so they cascade in instead of appearing as one rigid block.
- **Why**: A cascade guides the eye and feels less abrupt than a single hard snap.
- **Do / Don't**: Do stagger siblings by a few frames. Don't mount a whole grid/list with one simultaneous pop.
- **Audit check**: grep for `staggerChildren`, `animation-delay`, `transition-delay` on list/card groups; flag lists that enter all at once with no delay offset.
- **Restyle fix**: Add an incremental per-item delay (index × small step) to list/card enter animations.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W2-04 Reuse one small set of easing curves everywhere  (src: easing-curves)
- **Rule**: Use the same handful of curves for button press, card cascade, and sheet open so motion feels coherent rather than stitched together.
- **Why**: Consistent easing is a large part of perceived coherence.
- **Do / Don't**: Do define easing tokens and reuse them. Don't scatter one-off cubic-bezier values across components.
- **Audit check**: grep and count distinct `cubic-bezier(...)` / easing values across the codebase; many unique values with no shared tokens is a flag.
- **Restyle fix**: Define 2–3 easing tokens (CSS variables or Tailwind theme) and replace ad-hoc curves with them.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

## Web patterns: Fundamentals

### W2-05 Group related items with shared visual properties  (src: gestalt-laws)
- **Rule**: Items sharing color, shape, or size look like one set, so recoloring rows can carve a uniform grid into Navigation, Content, and Actions. Line related controls up along one axis.
- **Why**: Brains group by similarity and continuity automatically.
- **Do / Don't**: Do style related items alike and align them on one line. Don't scatter them at mixed offsets or angles.
- **Audit check**: In screenshots, check that items in one function share color/size/shape and that controls share a left or center edge; flag inconsistent offsets among siblings.
- **Restyle fix**: Unify styling of related items and snap them to one alignment axis.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: hierarchy, alignment, layout

### W2-06 Wrap distant related elements in a shared container  (src: gestalt-laws)
- **Rule**: When proximity alone cannot group elements, a shared border or card (common region) signals belonging without moving them closer, such as settings wrapped in cards.
- **Why**: A shared region groups items even when they sit far apart.
- **Do / Don't**: Do wrap loosely placed related elements in a bordered card. Don't rely on whitespace alone to group dispersed items.
- **Audit check**: Look for related controls across a page with no wrapping container (`border`, `rounded`, `bg-*` card wrapper) and ambiguous group boundaries.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a bordered/filled card around each logical group.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: cards, borders, layout

### W2-07 Dim and blur the background behind a modal  (src: gestalt-laws)
- **Rule**: Blur and dim what is behind a dialog so the dialog stands out as the focal figure against the background.
- **Why**: Figure-ground separation makes the dialog feel deliberate rather than floating.
- **Do / Don't**: Do dim/blur the backdrop. Don't show a modal over an undimmed page where it competes with the content.
- **Audit check**: grep modal/dialog markup for backdrop/overlay: look for `bg-black/*`, `backdrop-blur`, `::backdrop`; flag dialogs with no scrim.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a scrim (e.g. semi-transparent dark overlay) and optional `backdrop-filter: blur()` behind the modal.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: depth, hierarchy

### W2-08 Derive spacing and type scales from one ratio  (src: golden-ratio)
- **Rule**: Multiply a base unit by 1.618 for spacing (8 → 13 → 21 → 34 → 55) and type (body 16px, subheading 26px, heading 42px, display 68px), rounded to whole pixels.
- **Why**: One ratio gives gaps and hierarchy a common rhythm and removes guesswork.
- **Do / Don't**: Do derive scales from one base and ratio, rounded to usable pixels. Don't choose sizes ad hoc or force the ratio against an existing 8px grid.
- **Audit check**: Collect distinct font sizes and spacing values (`text-[..px]`, `gap-[..]`, `p-[..]`); flag many one-off values with no consistent step ratio.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace arbitrary sizes with a tokenized scale such as 8/13/21/34/55 and 16/26/42/68, rounded to your grid. (On conflict the playbook wins: see B-15/B-17, base-unit multiples.)
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: spacing, typography, hierarchy

### W2-09 Split layouts roughly 62% / 38%  (src: golden-ratio)
- **Rule**: Divide the screen around the golden ratio, about 62% / 38%, giving the larger panel to primary content and the smaller to secondary actions.
- **Why**: Unequal proportions create clear primary/secondary hierarchy and balanced composition.
- **Do / Don't**: Do give primary content the larger share. Don't force the ratio where real content needs otherwise.
- **Audit check**: In two-pane layouts, check widths (`w-[62%]`, `grid-cols-[1.618fr_1fr]`, flex ratios); flag 50/50 splits where one pane is clearly secondary.
- **Restyle fix**: Set the primary pane to ~62% and secondary to ~38% of the width.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: layout, hierarchy

### W2-10 Use a 12-column grid with clear column ratios  (src: grid-system)
- **Rule**: Start from a 12-column grid because 12 divides into halves, thirds, quarters, and sixths. Structure pages with ratios like 4:8 (sidebar + content), 6:6 (even split), or 3:9 (narrow nav + wide canvas).
- **Why**: A shared grid lets nearly any layout map cleanly and read as intentional.
- **Do / Don't**: Do anchor elements to shared column edges using clean ratios. Don't use arbitrary widths where 4:8 or 6:6 already fits.
- **Audit check**: grep `grid-cols-12`, `col-span-*`; flag hard-coded pixel widths on layout blocks and edges that do not align to a column.
- **Restyle fix**: Rebuild layout blocks as column spans on a 12-col grid.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: layout, alignment, spacing

### W2-11 Choose gutter width to match the intended mood  (src: grid-system)
- **Rule**: Gutters set tone: 8px reads dense and technical, 24px balanced and clean, 40px editorial and premium.
- **Why**: Gap size changes density perception as much as columns do.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use tight gutters for dense dashboards and wide for editorial. Don't use one default gap regardless of context.
- **Audit check**: Inspect `gap-*`/`column-gap` values per layout type; flag dense data UIs with 40px gutters or editorial pages with 8px.
- **Restyle fix**: Set gutters to ~8px (dense), ~24px (general), or ~40px (editorial) to match the page's purpose.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: spacing, layout

### W2-12 Collapse the grid at breakpoints: 12 → 6 → 4 → 1  (src: grid-system)
- **Rule**: Reduce columns per breakpoint: 12 for desktop, 6 for tablet, 4 for large phones, and one stacked column at the smallest widths.
- **Why**: Cutting columns step by step lets content reflow neatly.
- **Do / Don't**: Do cut the column count at each breakpoint. Don't keep the desktop column count on small screens.
- **Audit check**: grep for responsive prefixes (`sm:`, `md:`, `lg:` with `grid-cols-*`, `@media` column rules); flag fixed multi-column grids with no mobile fallback to a single column.
- **Restyle fix**: Add breakpoint variants that reduce columns and stack to 1 column on small screens.

### W2-13 Establish the grid before breaking it on purpose  (src: grid-system)
- **Rule**: Intentional grid breaks (a full-width hero bleed or a pull quote pushed into the margin) only read as deliberate against an otherwise orderly grid.
- **Why**: Emphasis through contrast needs an established order to contrast with.
- **Do / Don't**: Do break the grid sparingly for emphasis. Don't break it before the base alignment is solid.
- **Audit check**: Check for negative margins / absolute offsets (`-mx-*`, `w-screen`) used in pages where other content is misaligned.

- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: layout, alignment, craft

### W2-14 Keep gradients within about 60° of hue  (src: gradient-design)
- **Rule**: Blend neighboring hues (teal → cyan) rather than opposites (orange → blue), which create a muddy gray middle. Stay within 60° of hue travel and keep lightness moving in one direction to avoid banding.
- **Why**: Wide hue travel and lightness reversals make gradients look cheap.
- **Do / Don't**: Do keep hue travel under 60° and lightness monotonic. Don't blend opposite hues or go dark → light → dark.
- **Audit check**: grep `linear-gradient(`, `from-* via-* to-*`, `radial-gradient(` and compare end-stop hues; flag complementary endpoints.
- **Restyle fix**: Pick end colors on neighboring hues; adjust stops so lightness changes in one direction.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: color, craft

### W2-15 Use gradients as ambient glow, not as full surfaces behind text  (src: gradient-design)
- **Rule**: Put a soft radial glow behind content instead of a full-bleed linear wash over it, and keep body copy off the gradient's blended middle band.
- **Why**: Contrast can't be predicted where the colors blend.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use gradients as atmosphere behind content. Don't set body text straight onto a gradient's middle.
- **Audit check**: Find text elements over gradient backgrounds (`bg-gradient-*` ancestors) and measure contrast at the mid-transition area.
- **Restyle fix**: Move the gradient to a background glow layer or put text on a solid/scrimmed surface.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: color, contrast

### W2-16 Add 2–3% grain over gradients  (src: gradient-design)
- **Rule**: Overlay about 2–3% noise on gradients to hide banding on poor displays and add texture; check on a low-end screen.
- **Why**: Smooth gradients band visibly on cheap displays; grain disguises it.
- **Do / Don't**: Do add faint noise and preview it on a weak display. Don't release big smooth gradients without checking for banding.
- **Audit check**: Look for large gradients with no noise overlay (no noise SVG/PNG, `feTurbulence`, `mix-blend` texture layer); inspect screenshots for visible banding.
- **Restyle fix**: Overlay a low-opacity (~2–3%) noise texture on large gradient areas.

### W2-17 Optically size round icons 5–8% larger than square ones  (src: icon-design-rules)
- **Rule**: Round and organic glyphs should be drawn roughly 5–8% bigger than boxy ones so they look equal; identical pixel boxes make circles seem undersized.
- **Why**: What the eye perceives as size is not the same as the measured size.
- **Do / Don't**: Do enlarge circular icons by about 5–8%. Don't rely only on matching bounding-box dimensions.
- **Audit check**: In icon sets, compare visual weight of circle-based vs square icons in the same row; check SVG viewBox fill proportions.
- **Restyle fix**: Scale circular/organic icons by 5–8% within the same container.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: icons, craft

### W2-18 Align icons to a 24px pixel grid (16px when dense) in a uniform container  (src: icon-design-rules)
- **Rule**: Draw icon shapes on a 24px grid, or 16px in compact UI, to avoid sub-pixel blur; put every icon in an identically sized container so toolbars look even.
- **Why**: Fractional-pixel offsets soften edges, and uneven boxes make toolbars look ragged.
- **Do / Don't**: Do use one container size and a pixel grid. Don't allow icons to drift in arbitrary, mismatched sizes.
- **Audit check**: grep icon size classes/props (`size-*`, `w-* h-*`, `width=`/`height=` on svg); flag a mix of sizes beyond 16/24 within one toolbar.
- **Restyle fix**: Standardize icon containers to 24px (16px dense) and normalize SVG viewBox to the grid.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: icons, alignment, craft

### W2-19 Keep one 2px stroke weight across the icon set  (src: icon-design-rules)
- **Rule**: Use one 2px line weight on every icon; mixing weights makes the set look pieced together from several libraries.
- **Why**: Uniform line weight is the quickest giveaway of a polished icon family.
- **Do / Don't**: Do keep one line weight across the whole family. Don't mix weights or pull from different icon libraries.
- **Audit check**: grep `stroke-width=`/`strokeWidth` values and imports from multiple icon libraries; flag values other than a single standard.
- **Restyle fix**: Set a uniform `stroke-width` (2) or replace icons from mismatched libraries.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: icons, craft

### W2-20 Commit to fill or outline for the whole icon set  (src: icon-design-rules)
- **Rule**: Pick one approach for the whole set, either filled or outlined, instead of deciding icon by icon.
- **Why**: Random mixing lacks any unifying visual logic.
- **Do / Don't**: Do settle on one style, optionally an intentional active/inactive scheme used consistently. Don't alternate fill and outline arbitrarily.
- **Audit check**: Review icon imports/variants (e.g. `Solid` vs `Outline` suffixes, `fill=` mixed with `stroke=`) for inconsistent use in the same context.

- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: icons, craft

- **Rule**: Check a field when focus leaves it rather than on submit or each keystroke; once it has errored, switch that field to live validation so the error clears the moment it is fixed.

- **Rule**: Use a green check to tell users a field is correct, not only when it is wrong.

- **Audit check**: Review the section order of landing pages; flag proof placed at the bottom and solution before problem.
- **Restyle fix**: Reorder sections to Hero → Proof → Problem → Solution → CTA.

### W2-75 Keep the hero to a headline, subhead, and one CTA  (src: landing-page-skeleton)

### W2-78 Prefer cursor pagination when data changes often  (src: pagination)
- **Rule**: Offset pagination drifts when rows are inserted or deleted, showing duplicates or skipping items; cursor pagination anchors to a specific row and stays stable.
- **Why**: Stable lists avoid duplicates and skips.

### W2-79 Truncate page links to first, last, current, and neighbors  (src: pagination)

### W2-80 Persist the page in the URL and restore scroll on return  (src: pagination)

## Web patterns: Interactions

### W2-81 Never hide primary actions behind hover  (src: hover-trap)
- **Rule**: Touch has no hover, so the first tap becomes a sticky hover that freezes revealed actions. Hover may surface extras only, and hover-only actions need a touch-reachable home (in the card, behind a swipe, or in a bottom sheet).
- **Why**: Touch users can't reach hover-only actions.
- **Do / Don't**: Do keep every primary action tappable. Don't hide primary actions behind hover.
- **Audit check**: grep `group-hover:opacity-100`, `:hover` reveal of buttons (`opacity-0 group-hover:`) for essential actions.
- **Restyle fix**: Make the actions always visible or move them into a menu/sheet on touch.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: mobile, interaction-cost, buttons

### W2-82 Wrap hover styles in @media (hover: hover) and use pointer: coarse  (src: hover-trap)
- **Rule**: Test pointer capability with `@media (hover: hover)` instead of user-agent sniffing, and use `pointer: coarse` to enlarge controls for thumbs.
- **Why**: Mouse-equipped tablets keep full hover behavior while touch-only devices dodge sticky hover.
- **Do / Don't**: Do rely on media queries for hover and pointer. Don't infer touch from the user-agent string.
- **Audit check**: grep `navigator.userAgent` touch sniffing, `:hover` rules outside `@media (hover: hover)`, and `pointer: coarse` usage.
- **Restyle fix**: Wrap hover styles in `@media (hover: hover)` and add `pointer: coarse` sizing.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: mobile, interaction-cost

### W2-83 Pad touch targets to 44px while keeping icons about 20px  (src: hover-trap)
- **Rule**: A 20px icon looks fine in design review yet is hard to hit; pad the hit area to 44px while keeping the glyph small.
- **Why**: Undersized targets lead to taps landing in the wrong place.
- **Do / Don't**: Do expand the tap area to 44px. Don't let the icon's size set the target size.
- **Audit check**: grep icon buttons with `w-5 h-5`/`size-5` and no padding; computed button size under 44px.
- **Restyle fix**: Add padding (e.g. `p-3`) or `min-w-11 min-h-11` to icon buttons.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: mobile, accessibility, buttons

### W2-84 Interpolate live cursor positions between server ticks  (src: live-cursors)
- **Rule**: The server streams about 10 positions per second while the screen redraws at 60fps; interpolate between ticks so cursors glide instead of teleporting.
- **Why**: Raw tick-rate rendering looks jumpy.
- **Do / Don't**: Do interpolate positions each frame. Don't render at raw server tick rate.
- **Audit check**: Check cursor rendering for lerp/spring/`requestAnimationFrame` smoothing versus direct position assignment on each message.
- **Restyle fix**: Add interpolation or a spring to cursor movement.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: motion, craft

### W2-85 Hash user color from a stable ID and show presence  (src: live-cursors)
- **Rule**: Derive each collaborator's color from a hash of their ID so it persists across sessions, and show an avatar stack with an overflow counter (e.g. three faces, then +5) to signal presence.
- **Why**: Stable identity cues are tracked peripherally; presence is felt before anyone speaks.
- **Do / Don't**: Do hash IDs to colors and show an avatar stack with overflow. Don't assign random colors per session.
- **Audit check**: grep `Math.random()` used for user colors; check for an avatar group with `+N` overflow.
- **Restyle fix**: Replace random color assignment with an ID hash and add a stacked avatar presence indicator.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: color, craft

### W2-86 Lock selected elements and outline them in the editor's color  (src: live-cursors)
- **Rule**: Selecting an element should lock it and outline it in the selector's color, preventing two people editing the same shape.
- **Why**: Stops clashing edits.
- **Do / Don't**: Do lock and badge selected elements. Don't permit simultaneous edits of one element.
- **Audit check**: Check collaborative selection code for lock state broadcast and a colored outline/badge on locked elements.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a lock indicator with the editor's color on selected elements.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: states, interaction-cost

### W2-87 Offer follow mode for live design review  (src: live-cursors)
- **Rule**: Let a user click another user's avatar to bind their viewport to that user's pans and zooms, replacing screen share.
- **Why**: It makes live review in-product.
- **Do / Don't**: Do bind the viewport on avatar click. Don't force screen sharing for review.
- **Audit check**: Look for avatar click handlers that subscribe to another user's viewport state.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a follow action to avatars with a visible "following" state.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: interaction-cost, navigation

## Web patterns: Inputs

### W3-01 Show password requirements live as the user types  (src: password-field-ux)
- **Rule**: Show the requirement list from the start, check items off as they pass, and grow a strength meter with each keystroke instead of explaining rules after a failed submit.
- **Why**: Guidance while typing spares users a penalty after the fact.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Update a checklist and strength meter with every keypress. Don't: Conceal requirements until submission, then display red errors.
- **Audit check**: On password/signup inputs, look for an onChange/oninput-driven checklist or meter; flag forms where rules text appears only in an error state after submit.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a requirements list under the field bound to the input value, tick items as they pass, and add a strength bar.

### W3-02 Let users reveal the password and use paste or generated passwords  (src: password-field-ux)
- **Rule**: Give password fields an eye toggle to unmask, allow pasting so password managers can fill long credentials, and ideally offer a one-tap generated password. Judge strength by length/entropy rather than a mandatory-symbol tally.
- **Why**: Masked dots hide silent typos, and blocking paste pushes users toward weaker passwords.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Offer a visibility toggle, allow paste, and offer a generated password. Don't: Block paste or treat a capital-plus-symbol checkbox as proof of strength.
- **Audit check**: grep for onPaste with preventDefault / onpaste="return false" on password inputs; check for type toggle between password and text; check for autocomplete="new-password".
- **Restyle fix**: Remove paste blockers, add an eye toggle button (aria-label, aria-pressed) that switches input type, and add a generate-password action.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: forms, accessibility, interaction-cost

### W3-03 Make sliders fillable, fully draggable and keyboard operable  (src: range-sliders)
- **Rule**: Fill the track up to the thumb for an instant value read, and make the whole row draggable, not a 4px hairline. Arrows step by one; Home/End jump to extremes.
- **Why**: Precise dragging is hard, and a thin unfilled track leaves the value to guesswork.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Provide a filled track, a row-wide drag target, and arrow/Home/End support. Don't: Depend on a 4px hairline as the sole target or ship mouse-only sliders.
- **Audit check**: Inspect custom sliders for a filled-range element, track height ~4px with no enlarged padding/hit area, and onKeyDown handling of ArrowLeft/ArrowRight/Home/End (or native input[type=range]).
- **Restyle fix**: Add a fill element sized to the value, pad the track hit area to the full row height, and add key handlers plus role="slider" with aria-valuenow/min/max.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: forms, accessibility, interaction-cost

### W3-04 Snap sliders to steps and show the value above the thumb  (src: range-sliders)
- **Rule**: Snap to steps with visible ticks so values like 47.3 never appear. Show the exact value over the thumb while dragging; use a two-thumb band for min/max cases like price.
- **Why**: The number appears where the eye already looks, and values stay tidy.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Use stepped snapping and a live value tooltip over the thumb. Don't: Keep a slider continuous if users need clean numbers.
- **Audit check**: Check for a step attribute/value on range inputs and a value readout near the thumb during drag; price filters should use a two-thumb range.
- **Restyle fix**: Set step on the slider, add tick marks, render a tooltip bound to the thumb position, and use a dual-thumb band for ranges.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: forms, interaction-cost, states

### W3-05 Make star ratings preview on hover and restore on leave  (src: star-rating)
- **Rule**: Light up the stars up to the pointer while hovering, as a preview before committing. Hold this hover state apart from the saved rating so leaving without clicking restores it.
- **Why**: People see what they are about to submit rather than finding out afterwards.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Preview while hovering and return to the saved value on mouse leave. Don't: Let the display stay frozen on the last hovered star.
- **Audit check**: Look for separate hover and value state (e.g. hoverValue vs value) and an onMouseLeave that resets hover; flag rating widgets with only onClick.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a hover state driving fill, and clear it on mouseleave so the display falls back to the saved rating.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: forms, states, interaction-cost

### W3-06 Render fractional stars for averages and stagger the fill  (src: star-rating)
- **Rule**: Show averages as partial fills: 4.4 becomes four whole stars and a fifth cut at 44%, never rounded up. On commit, fill left to right with ~30ms stagger per star.
- **Why**: Rounding up overstates quality, and an all-at-once pop feels dull.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Clip the final star to the fractional percentage and sweep the fill with ~30ms stagger. Don't: Round averages upward or pop every star together.
- **Audit check**: Check average displays for Math.round/Math.ceil on rating values and for a width/clip-path percentage on the last star; check transition-delay steps of about 30ms per star.
- **Restyle fix**: Use a width or clip-path percentage on an overlay star layer and add per-index transition delay of ~30ms.

### W3-07 Write a descriptive search placeholder and show recent searches  (src: search-experience-system)
- **Rule**: Write a placeholder that says what is searchable (e.g. "Search by name, SKU, or brand") rather than a bare "Search". Show recent searches the moment the field gains focus so one tap refills it.
- **Why**: The placeholder is the first onboarding and recent items cut friction to zero.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Use descriptive placeholder text and surface recent searches on focus. Don't: Ship a bare "Search" placeholder with an empty focus state.
- **Audit check**: grep placeholder="Search" (exact, bare) on search inputs; check for an onFocus-driven recent-searches list.
- **Restyle fix**: Rewrite the placeholder to name searchable fields and render a recent-searches panel on focus.

### W3-08 Rank autocomplete by popularity and support keyboard navigation  (src: search-experience-system)
- **Rule**: Rank suggestions by clicks, not alphabetically, with category badges; three precise hits beat ten noisy ones. Arrows navigate, Enter selects, Escape closes, and the focus ring stays visible.
- **Why**: Popularity ordering surfaces probable targets, and hidden focus breaks keyboard use.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Rank by popularity, add category badges, support full keyboard control and a visible focus ring. Don't: Order suggestions alphabetically or strip the focus outline.
- **Audit check**: Check for onKeyDown handling ArrowUp/ArrowDown/Enter/Escape, role="listbox"/"option" with aria-activedescendant, and outline:none / focus:outline-none without a replacement focus style.
- **Restyle fix**: Add key handlers and ARIA combobox roles, sort suggestions by usage score, and restore a visible focus-visible ring.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: forms, accessibility, navigation

### W3-09 Make empty search results a way forward  (src: search-experience-system)
- **Rule**: When a search finds nothing, present popular searches, category shortcuts or alternative spellings, helping users recover just as they would otherwise abandon.
- **Why**: An empty dead end is where people bounce.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Give suggestions and category links when results are empty. Don't: Leave only a plain "No matches" line.
- **Audit check**: Find empty/no-results branches in search components and check they render more than a single text line (links, suggestions).
- **Restyle fix**: Add popular searches, category chips and a spell-suggestion line to the empty state.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: states, copy, interaction-cost

### W3-10 Chunk long forms into steps with visible progress  (src: stepper-wizard)
- **Rule**: Split long forms into meaningful groups (Personal, Shipping, Payment, Review), not arbitrary counts: three fields are easy, twelve cause exhausting scrolling. Add one progress cue: bar, numbered dots or labels.
- **Why**: Chunking reduces mental load, and visible progress shows the finish nearing.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Group steps by context and include a progress indicator. Don't: Pile twelve fields into one scrolling wall or split at arbitrary counts.
- **Audit check**: Count inputs per form/page (flag around 12 or more in one view); check for a stepper/progress element in multi-step flows.
- **Restyle fix**: Break the form into semantic steps and add a progress bar, dots, or labeled steps.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: forms, layout, interaction-cost

### W3-11 Validate inside each step and keep entered data  (src: stepper-wizard)
- **Rule**: Validate fields within their own step when Next is clicked, show inline errors immediately instead of rejecting at the final screen, and focus the first invalid field. Persist state on every step change so Back and refresh keep what was entered.
- **Why**: Losing a form or surfacing step-one errors on step four loses the user.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Validate per step, show inline errors, and persist data across steps and refresh. Don't: Surface early errors at the end or discard input on Back/refresh.
- **Audit check**: Check each step validates on Next-click (not via a disabled Next; see W1-94), keeps entered data, and flags and focuses the first invalid field; check persistence (localStorage/sessionStorage/URL/state store) across steps.
- **Restyle fix**: Keep Next enabled, validate the step on click with a per-step schema, keep entered data, show inline messages and focus the first invalid field (see W1-94); persist form state on each step change.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: forms, states, interaction-cost

### W3-12 Build toggles with fixed proportions and a keyboard-accessible semantics  (src: toggle-anatomy)
- **Rule**: Make the rail 2x the knob's diameter, padded by the knob's radius so it stays centred in both states. Space toggles when focused; show a visible focus ring; expose the state through either role="switch" with aria-checked on a button, or a native checkbox using its own checked state (never both).
- **Why**: Steady proportions look polished, and proper semantics let keyboard and screen-reader users operate it.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Use a button with role="switch" and aria-checked, or a native checkbox with its native checked state; add Space support and a visible focus ring. Don't: Ship a toggle that reacts only to mouse clicks, or put aria-checked on a native checkbox.
- **Audit check**: grep custom toggles for role="switch" + aria-checked on a button, or a native input type=checkbox without aria-checked (not a bare div onClick), and focus-visible styles; check rail width ≈ 2× knob size.
- **Restyle fix**: Use either a button with role="switch" and aria-checked, or a native checkbox relying on its checked state; add focus-visible ring and correct rail/knob sizing.
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: forms, accessibility, craft

### W3-13 Animate toggle flips over ~250ms and handle async state  (src: toggle-anatomy)
- **Rule**: Transition rail color, knob translateX, knob shadow and label together over ~250ms ease-out. Async: flip optimistically, show a knob loader while pending, revert with shake and error toast on failure.
- **Why**: An abrupt jump looks broken, and a switch without a spinner seems stuck during a request.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Morph all four properties together, with a pending spinner and failure rollback. Don't: Snap the knob instantly or leave network status unclear.
- **Audit check**: Check toggle CSS for transition on background-color and transform with ~250ms ease-out; flag toggles with no transition; check async toggles for pending and error rollback handling.
- **Restyle fix**: Add transition-all duration-[250ms] ease-out to rail/knob, add pending spinner state and rollback on error.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: forms, motion, states

## Web patterns: Fundamentals

### W3-14 Space items inside a group more tightly than between groups  (src: proximity-rule)
- **Rule**: Close elements read as one group, so keep spacing within groups tighter than between them: about 12px between related form fields, about 40px between sections. Equal spacing flattens hierarchy.
- **Why**: Viewers infer relationships purely from distance.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Keep spacing tight inside groups and larger at section breaks. Don't: Space every element identically.
- **Audit check**: Compare gap/margin values within and between groups (e.g. gap-3 within vs gap-10 between); flag forms where field and section gaps are identical.
- **Restyle fix**: Set related fields to ~12px spacing and section breaks to ~40px. (On conflict the playbook wins: see A-16, label 12px / next field 32px.)
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: spacing, hierarchy, layout

### W3-15 Group nav and toolbar items by function and let whitespace do the dividing  (src: proximity-rule)
- **Rule**: Group controls by function (navigate, actions, system) and split long lists like eight sidebar links into labeled groups such as Dashboard, Management and Account. Use whitespace for grouping before reaching for borders, boxes or dividers.
- **Why**: Spacing and labeled groups make flat lists scannable without extra lines.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Group by meaning and rely on spacing first. Don't: Use boxes and dividers where a larger gap says the same thing.
- **Audit check**: Check sidebars/toolbars for a single evenly spaced list of many items and for border/divider elements between every group.
- **Restyle fix**: Split long nav lists into labeled sections and swap dividers for larger gaps.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: spacing, navigation, layout, hierarchy

### W3-16 Stack size, color, contrast, whitespace and weight to create one focal point  (src: visual-hierarchy)
- **Rule**: Make the primary element about 2x body size with more padding than neighbours, supported by weight: 800 headings, 400 body, 300 captions. Combine properties; none carries a layout alone.
- **Why**: Stacked cues create a clear entry point and reading order.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Make the headline about double body size, combining size with weight, contrast and spacing. Don't: Depend on size alone or give everything equal emphasis.
- **Audit check**: Compare heading to body font sizes (ratio near 2x), font-weight spread across headings/body/captions, and padding of the hero versus secondary items.
- **Restyle fix**: Scale the headline to ~2x body, set weights 800/400/300 by role, and give the hero extra padding.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: hierarchy, typography, spacing

### W3-17 Reserve one accent color for the single key action  (src: visual-hierarchy)
- **Rule**: Keep the interface neutral; reserve one accent for the one action you want taken. Contrast pairs: bold white headings over muted copy, filled primary beside ghost secondary.
- **Why**: Many colors turn hierarchy into noise.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Use one accent for the primary action and ghost-style secondaries. Don't: Give each element its own color.
- **Audit check**: Count distinct accent/hue classes in a view; check for more than one filled primary-style button per view.
- **Restyle fix**: Neutralize secondary elements, make secondary buttons ghost/outline, and keep the accent only on the primary CTA. (On conflict the playbook wins: see E-31, avoid pure white; use off-white for headings.)
- **Severity**: high ; **Tags**: color, hierarchy, buttons

### W3-18 Isolate one element against a uniform baseline  (src: von-restorff)
- **Rule**: Make one item differ: enlarge the pricing target with a "Most Popular" badge and dim others, or lift a lone CTA with color, scale and glow over quiet nav links.
- **Why**: Visually distinct items get noticed and remembered; contrast needs a uniform baseline.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Set the one target action apart through scale, color and a badge among quiet neighbours. Don't: Offer identical options and expect users to choose the intended one.
- **Audit check**: In pricing/option card sets check that one card differs in scale/badge/elevation; flag sets where all cards are identical or where several are highlighted.
- **Restyle fix**: Pick one target card/CTA, add scale, badge and glow, and mute the rest.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: hierarchy, cards, buttons

### W3-19 Limit emphasis to one element and never rely on color alone  (src: von-restorff)
- **Rule**: Highlight one element per view (featured plan, main CTA or form button); two or three cancel each other out. Add scale, elevation and glow to color so color-blind users notice.
- **Why**: Competing emphasis cancels isolation, and color-only contrast fails some users.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Emphasise one element and pair color with scale or elevation. Don't: Highlight several items or depend on color alone.
- **Audit check**: Count emphasized elements per view (primary buttons, highlighted cards); check whether the emphasized state differs by more than hue (size, shadow, badge).
- **Restyle fix**: Demote extra highlights and add scale/elevation/badge to the one standout.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: hierarchy, accessibility, color

### W3-20 Put the most important items at the start and end of a sequence  (src: serial-position)
- **Rule**: U-shaped recall: navbars begin with logo, end with primary CTA; landing pages open with the best USP, close with proof (testimonials, metrics, guarantees); onboarding hooks first, pays off last.
- **Why**: Items in the middle of a sequence are forgotten.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Anchor key items at the start and end of lists or flows. Don't: Hide important CTAs or information midway.
- **Audit check**: In navbars check the primary CTA is the last item and the logo is first; on landing pages check the USP is at the top and flag proof buried mid-page (proof directly after the hero is fine; see W2-74).
- **Restyle fix**: Reorder nav so the primary CTA/logo bookend it, and move buried mid-page proof to the hero-adjacent slot or the page close (see W2-74).
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: hierarchy, navigation, layout

### W3-21 Engineer a deliberate peak and a strong ending in flows  (src: peak-end-rule)
- **Rule**: Users judge a flow by its most intense moment and its final moment, so design at least one intentional peak (surprise upgrade, free perk, delight) and end on a celebration, not a flat confirmation. One delight outweighs five neutral steps.
- **Why**: Memory stores the peak and the end rather than the average.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Add a peak partway through and celebrate on the last screen. Don't: Spread effort evenly while neglecting the finish.
- **Audit check**: Review the final screen of each flow (success/confirmation pages) for a plain text-only state; check for any celebratory element or next-step.
- **Restyle fix**: Upgrade the success screen with a celebratory visual, clear summary and next action.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: states, copy, craft

### W3-22 Audit the last interaction of every flow for friction  (src: peak-end-rule)
- **Rule**: A broken, error-ridden or dead-end last step ruins how an otherwise smooth experience is remembered, so verify that each flow finishes without friction.
- **Why**: The ending carries outsized weight in what users recall.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Review the final interaction of every flow. Don't: Let a flow close on friction, errors or a dead end.
- **Audit check**: Inspect last-step handlers for unhandled errors, missing success state, and screens with no next action or link.
- **Restyle fix**: Add error recovery to the final step and a next-step CTA to the confirmation.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: states, interaction-cost

### W3-23 Use an open loop only for outcomes users want  (src: zeigarnik-effect)
- **Rule**: A visible gap (progress stuck at 80%, one unchecked onboarding box) keeps an unfinished task in mind, but only for goals users care about, like profile completion, not fake progress.
- **Why**: Unfinished tasks stay in active memory; completed ones are dropped.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Show noticeable remaining progress tied to a goal the user wants. Don't: Close every loop at 100% or invent progress, such as a reading bar in a marketing email.
- **Audit check**: Check onboarding/profile components for a progress meter with remaining items; flag progress bars on non-task content.
- **Restyle fix**: Add a profile/onboarding completion meter with the next unchecked step highlighted.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: states, interaction-cost, copy

## Web patterns: Surfaces

### W3-24 Give cards generous padding, radius and a clear type hierarchy  (src: perfect-card)
- **Rule**: Generous padding signals intent: use 40px instead of a cramped 12px, plus a 24px border-radius. Title at weight 600, ~38px; body smaller at ~55% opacity so the title wins.
- **Why**: Spacing and type account for most of the difference between cheap-looking and premium cards.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Use ~40px padding, ~24px radius, a 600-weight title and ~55% opacity description. Don't: Squeeze content to the edges with tiny padding, near-zero radius or equal title/body weight.
- **Audit check**: Check card classes for p-3/12px padding with rounded-none/sm, and title/body with the same font-weight and opacity.

### W3-25 Layer a tight and a soft shadow plus a ~12% hairline border on cards  (src: perfect-card)

### W3-26 Give clickable cards a hover lift  (src: perfect-card)

### W3-27 Build elevation from three layered shadows and use it as hierarchy  (src: shadow-elevation)

### W3-29 Build depth from background values and a hairline, not shadows  (src: reverse-engineered-linear)
- **Rule**: For a flat engineered look, skip shadows; use three background tones (base, surface, raised) divided by a 1px border of 8% white. Hover shifts surface value, not elevation.

- **Rule**: Compact UIs signal competence: 13px text, 32px rows, -1% tracking fit 14 issues versus 8. One indigo accent on selected row and primary button; status as grey icons, not pills.

### W3-31 Align labels left and numbers right on a 4px grid  (src: reverse-engineered-linear)
- **Rule**: Use a 4px grid and 16px icons centred on the text line. Labels and ids align left; numbers and dates align right; nothing is centred.
- **Why**: Correct alignment goes unnoticed; wrong alignment stands out.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Left-align text, right-align numbers/dates, and centre icons with the text line. Don't: Centre table cells or let icons drift off the text.
- **Audit check**: Check table cells for text-center on data columns, numeric columns without text-right/tabular-nums, and spacing values off the 4px scale.

### W3-32 Fix z-index with position and stacking contexts, not bigger numbers  (src: z-index-mastery)
- **Rule**: z-index needs position (relative, absolute, fixed or sticky). A child can't escape its parent's stacking context, so 9999 cannot win what the parent lost; values rank siblings per context.
- **Why**: Escalating from 9999 to 99999 hides an unexpected stacking context.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Add position before z-index and locate the parent stacking context. Don't: Keep raising to z-index: 9999 to push a child over another branch.
- **Audit check**: grep z-index values at or above 999; flag z-index only on plain position: static block elements (z-index does apply to flex/grid children even when static, so exclude them); count distinct z-index values for an ad hoc scale.
- **Restyle fix**: Remove arbitrary huge values, add position: relative where needed and define a small z-index scale.
- **Severity**: medium ; **Tags**: layout, craft

### W3-33 Contain component layers with isolation: isolate  (src: z-index-mastery)
- **Rule**: isolation: isolate creates a new stacking context in one declaration, so inner layers can't leak out and collide with the page. DevTools' Layers panel shows the real 3D stack.
- **Why**: Containing stacking removes any need for global z-index escalation.
- **Do / Don't**: Do: Apply isolation: isolate to components with inner layering. Don't: Presume that a larger z-index always prevails.
- **Audit check**: Check components with internal z-index children for isolation: isolate / the isolate Tailwind class.
- **Restyle fix**: Add isolate to the component root.
- **Severity**: low ; **Tags**: layout, craft

- **Rule**: Hold a hover tooltip back for about 300ms so accidental cursor passes don't open it, and add an arrow pointing at the trigger so the target is clear.

- **Rule**: Near a viewport edge, show the tooltip on the opposite side to avoid clipping, and dismiss it on mouse leave, Escape, blur (focus leaving) and tapping elsewhere.

- **Rule**: Limit tooltip width to about 300px and a single sentence; a paragraph of documentation belongs elsewhere.

### W3-40 Prefer undo with a visible countdown over "Are you sure?" dialogs  (src: undo-ux)
- **Rule**: Run the action immediately and offer a timed undo with a visible clock (draining ring or bar) in place of confirmation dialogs. Keep the window long enough to react.

### W3-52 Drive scroll effects with CSS scroll and view timelines  (src: scroll-driven-animations)

- **Rule**: Add scroll timelines over `position: sticky` to create headers that shrink, reading-progress bars, and sidebars that change as the page scrolls.
