# Notice: third-party sources

ux-doctor's code, workflows and wording are MIT-licensed (see `LICENSE`). Its **rule data is derived from third-party work**; the MIT licence does not grant rights in those sources.

- **The UI/UX Playbook** (uxpeak, uxpeak.com): the 188 playbook rules (`A-01` … `H-18`) are paraphrased summaries with short quoted values (numbers, thresholds), written page by page by AI agents from a purchased copy of the PDF. The PDF, its page text and page renders are **not** in this repository. Page numbers cite PDF pages (printed page = PDF page − 1 from p.3). Rule severities, "audit checks" and "restyle fixes" are this project's own interpretation, not the playbook's. Items marked `(inference)` or `(from visual)` are not stated by the book.
- **Public UX pattern pages**: the 253 web rules (`W1-…` to `W5-…`) are short paraphrases of the key insights on publicly available UX pattern pages, each tagged with a topic slug (`web:<slug>`). The raw page text is **not** in this repository. This project is not affiliated with the authors of those pages and contains nothing from any commercial product.
- **Maintainer-added tips** (`W4-…`): short paraphrases of community design-engineering tips (for example measuring the loaded UI to size skeleton loaders), added by the maintainer; no copied text.
- **AI-look tells** (`W5-…`): short paraphrases, added by the maintainer, of published guidance on the generic look of AI-generated interfaces: Anthropic's `frontend-design` skill (github.com/anthropics/skills, Apache-2.0), Vercel's `design.md` (vercel.com/design.md), Adrian Krebs' "Scoring Show HN submissions for AI design patterns" (adriankrebs.ch), Ralabs' "AI Design Slop" review guide (ralabs.org) and Ad Astra Computing's Slop Taxonomy (github.com/Ad-Astra-Computing/ahd). No text is copied; thresholds, audit checks, restyle fixes and the `slop-scan.mjs` heuristics are this project's own. The rules flag unchosen defaults, not AI authorship. This project is not affiliated with those authors.
- **WCAG** thresholds (4.5:1, 3:1, 24px targets) are public W3C requirements, labelled as general practice where the sources don't state them.
